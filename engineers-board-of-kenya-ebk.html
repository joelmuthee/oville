Engineers Board of Kenya (EBK): What Building Owners Need to Know

Eng. Oville Team April 2026 7 min read

What EBK Is

The Engineers Board of Kenya (EBK) is the statutory regulator for the engineering profession in Kenya. It was established under the Engineers Act, 2011, which replaced the older Engineers Registration Act. EBK is the body that decides who can legally practice engineering in Kenya and what they are allowed to sign off on.

For building owners, developers, contractors and lenders, EBK matters for one simple reason: engineering work on your project must be signed off by an EBK-registered professional engineer for it to be legal, insurable, and approvable by the County.

What EBK Actually Does

EBK performs four core functions:

  1. Registration. Maintains the official register of graduate engineers, professional engineers, and consulting engineering firms.
  2. Licensing. Issues annual practising licences. An engineer can be registered but not licensed for the current year, which means they cannot legally take on new work.
  3. Discipline. Investigates complaints against engineers, sanctions misconduct, and can strike off offenders.
  4. Standards. Sets minimum standards for engineering education, professional development, and ethical practice.

Registration Categories

1. Graduate Engineer

Recent university graduate with an accredited engineering degree (typically B.Sc. or B.Eng. from a Kenyan university, or an equivalent recognised by EBK). A graduate engineer can be employed and work under supervision but cannot independently sign off on structural drawings, audit reports, or compliance certificates.

2. Professional Engineer (PE)

A graduate engineer who has completed at least three years of supervised practical experience, sat the Professional Interview, and been approved by the Board. A Professional Engineer can independently sign off on structural designs, audit reports, and compliance documents within their declared field of practice.

Letters: "Eng." before the name. Look for "Eng. Jane Wambui, PE" or similar.

3. Consulting Engineer

A Professional Engineer who has practiced for an additional period (typically 5+ years) at PE level and has been approved to run a consulting practice in their own right. Consulting Engineers can lead independent firms providing engineering services to the public.

4. Consulting Engineering Firm

A registered partnership or company that is licensed by EBK to offer engineering consultancy services. The firm must have at least one Consulting Engineer as a principal. Without firm-level registration, a single PE cannot legally trade as an engineering practice.

How to Verify an Engineer Is Registered

EBK publishes the register online. Before engaging any engineer or firm on your project:

  1. Go to www.ebk.go.ke
  2. Click on the "Register" or "Search Register" section
  3. Search by name, firm name, or registration number
  4. Confirm that both the engineer and the firm appear in the current year's register, with a valid licence
  5. Note the category of registration (PE, CE) and the field of practice (civil, structural, electrical, mechanical, etc.)

An engineer who is registered but not currently licensed cannot legally work on your project. Always check the licence year, not just the registration.

When EBK Sign-Off Is Mandatory

The Engineers Act and the various County by-laws require an EBK-registered Professional Engineer to sign off on:

  • Structural drawings submitted for County approval
  • Structural audit reports (especially those required under NCA notices)
  • Building condition reports for change-of-use applications
  • Foundation designs for buildings over a certain footprint or height (varies by County, typically two storeys or above)
  • Mechanical and electrical service designs above a certain capacity
  • Public infrastructure works (roads, bridges, water, sewerage)
  • Forensic engineering investigations for litigation or insurance

A County plans officer who receives drawings without an EBK stamp from a registered engineer is required to reject the submission. In practice, plans get processed without it sometimes, but the building owner remains liable.

EBK vs NCA: What Is the Difference?

People confuse these all the time. They regulate different things:

EBK NCA
Regulates engineers (people and firms)Regulates contractors and construction works
Engineers Act, 2011National Construction Authority Act, 2011
Issues PE and CE licencesIssues NCA contractor categories
Sanctions individual engineersHalts non-compliant construction sites
Required to design and sign drawingsRequired to build and project-manage

A compliant project needs both: an EBK-registered engineer to design and sign off, and an NCA-registered contractor to build.

Penalties for Non-Compliance

The Engineers Act provides for:

  • Fines of up to KES 5 million for individuals practising as engineers without registration
  • Imprisonment of up to 5 years for repeat offences or for impersonating a registered engineer
  • Strike-off for registered engineers found guilty of professional misconduct (taking responsibility for work they did not supervise, signing off on unsafe designs, etc.)
  • Vicarious liability for clients who knowingly engage unregistered engineers, particularly if a structural failure follows

Why This Matters for Buyers and Tenants

When you buy or lease a building in Kenya, the structural drawings and any compliance certificates should bear an EBK-registered engineer's name and stamp. If they do not, you face three risks:

  1. Insurance refusal. Some insurers will not pay structural-failure claims on buildings that were not designed by a registered engineer.
  2. NCA intervention. The Authority can issue a notice to vacate at any point if a building is found to have been constructed without compliant design oversight.
  3. Resale damage. A future buyer's lawyer will spot the gap during due diligence and either walk away or discount the price heavily.

EBK's Continuing Professional Development (CPD)

Registered engineers must complete a minimum number of CPD points per year to keep their licence current. The Board has tightened enforcement of this in recent years. CPD activities include attending recognised seminars, publishing technical papers, mentoring graduate engineers, and undertaking accredited short courses.

For clients, the relevance is: an engineer who has been continuously licensed without lapses for the past several years has demonstrably kept up with their CPD. Gaps in the licence year (registered but not licensed in 2023, for example) are worth asking about.
