Building Safety Audit Checklist: What to Check in a Kenyan Building

Eng. Oville Team April 2026 11 min read

Why This Checklist Exists

A safety audit is only as good as the items the auditor remembered to check. Even experienced engineers miss things when they audit from memory. This is the field checklist Oville auditors work through on every building, refined over hundreds of inspections in Nairobi and across Kenya.

It is intentionally broad. A full audit covers more than structure: fire, services, vertical transport, accessibility, and the boring-but-critical items like emergency lighting and signage. Skipping the non-structural items is how avoidable deaths happen in buildings that pass their structural test.

Section 1: Document Review (Before You Step On Site)

The audit starts in the office, not on site. Confirm you have or can obtain:

  • Title deed and current ownership records
  • Approved architectural drawings
  • Approved structural drawings (with EBK stamp)
  • Approved MEP drawings
  • Original design calculations (structural)
  • Approved geotechnical / soils report
  • Approval letters: County planning, NEMA, Fire Brigade, NCA
  • Concrete cube test results from construction
  • Steel mill certificates if available
  • Previous audit reports, if any
  • Maintenance logs (lift, generator, fire systems, water tanks)
  • Insurance valuation reports

Missing documents are themselves an audit finding. Most Kenyan buildings over 20 years old have lost their structural drawings. That changes the audit scope: more on-site measurement, more NDT, and a higher fee.

Section 2: External Walk-Around

Before entering, walk the full perimeter at ground level:

  • Foundation visible? Any movement, settlement cracks, exposed footings?
  • External wall condition: vertical cracks, diagonal cracks, water damage, efflorescence, missing render
  • Columns: any visible spalling, exposed rebar, corrosion staining?
  • Balconies and cantilevers: deflection, edge cracks, rebar exposure on soffits
  • Roof line: sagging ridge, uneven tile / sheeting alignment
  • Rainwater goods: blocked gutters, downpipes discharging onto foundations
  • Ground drainage: ponding water around the building, evidence of subsidence
  • Adjacent excavations or construction that could affect foundations
  • Trees within falling distance of the building
  • Boundary wall condition (its own structural element)

Section 3: Structural Audit Items

3.1 Columns

  • Visual inspection of every column on every floor where access permits
  • Vertical cracks, especially mid-height
  • Diagonal cracks (shear failure indicator)
  • Spalled corners, exposed rebar
  • Out-of-plumb measurements on suspect columns
  • Schmidt hammer readings on representative columns
  • Ferroscan on critical columns (transfer-supporting, corner, end-of-line)

3.2 Beams

  • Soffit cracks (especially mid-span, indicates flexural distress)
  • Diagonal shear cracks near supports
  • Deflection check (string-line or laser level)
  • Spalling on tension face
  • Cover thickness check with Ferroscan

3.3 Slabs

  • Soffit cracks, especially in two-way slab centres or near columns (punching shear)
  • Deflection measurement (string-line across span, measure max sag)
  • Top-side cracks over support beams
  • Wet patches indicating water ingress
  • Spalled rebar on soffits in older buildings

3.4 Walls (Load-Bearing)

  • Vertical cracks (settlement indicator)
  • Stepped cracks through brick or block work (differential settlement)
  • Diagonal cracks from openings
  • Bulging or out-of-plane displacement
  • Lintel cracking over openings

3.5 Foundations (Where Visible)

  • Inspect basement walls if any
  • Look for ground heave around the building
  • Trial pit if subsidence or expansive soil is suspected
  • Check soil type against original geotechnical report

Section 4: Fire Safety

4.1 Means of Escape

  • Two independent escape routes from every floor above first storey
  • Travel distances measured (maximum to a protected route)
  • Stair width, riser, going compliant with code
  • No reduction in stair width by goods storage
  • Doors swing in direction of escape
  • Push-bar exit hardware on final exit doors
  • No padlocks, chains, or locked emergency doors
  • Final exit discharges to a safe external place

4.2 Compartmentation

  • Fire-rated walls separating dwellings, between floors of mixed use, and around high-risk rooms
  • Fire doors with self-closers, properly maintained
  • No holes through fire-rated walls (services not fire-stopped)
  • Lift shaft, stair shaft, services riser fire-rated to required period

4.3 Detection, Alarm, Suppression

  • Smoke detectors in all corridors and escape routes
  • Fire alarm panel functional, manual call points at exits
  • Sprinklers where required by use class and floor area
  • Hose reels at correct spacing, water pressure verified
  • Fire mains and dry / wet risers (taller buildings)
  • Fire pump and tank (capacity, fuel level)
  • Fire extinguishers serviced within the last 12 months

4.4 Emergency Power and Lighting

  • Emergency lighting on all escape routes, tested under load
  • Exit signs illuminated and visible from any point on an escape route
  • Generator capacity sufficient for life-safety systems
  • Fuel supply for required runtime

Section 5: Electrical Systems

  • Main switchboard: labelled, current rating against load
  • Distribution boards: circuit labelling, RCD protection
  • Earthing: continuity tested, earth resistance recorded
  • Visible cabling not damaged, not run through escape routes uncovered
  • No overloaded sockets or daisy-chained extension leads
  • Generator changeover panel functional
  • Lightning protection on tall structures: continuity and earth resistance tested

Section 6: Plumbing and Drainage

  • Roof tanks: capacity, support, lid, vermin protection
  • Hot water systems: pressure relief valve functional
  • Foul drainage: gully traps in place, no smells, no blockages
  • Storm drainage: gutters and downpipes clear, discharge away from foundations
  • Sewer / septic capacity adequate for occupancy
  • Water supply pressure and quality (where on borehole)

Section 7: Vertical Transport (Lifts, Hoists)

  • Annual lift inspection certificate current
  • Lift maintenance contract in place and current
  • Emergency call-out functional
  • Cabin alarm and emergency lighting tested
  • Lift machine room: ventilation, no storage
  • Lift pit: dry, no debris
  • Manual evacuation procedure posted

Section 8: Means of Access and Universal Design

  • Approach to main entrance: ramped where possible
  • Door widths adequate for wheelchair access
  • Accessible toilet on at least the ground floor
  • Lift access between floors
  • Handrails on both sides of stairs
  • Tactile paving at top and bottom of stair flights

Section 9: External and Site Items

  • Boundary wall structural condition
  • Gates: rated for wind load, manual override if powered
  • Site lighting on escape paths
  • Fire brigade access: turning circle, hard standing, hydrant location
  • Refuse storage location: not blocking escape
  • Trees with potential to fall on the building

Section 10: Documentation and Compliance

  • Certificate of Occupation
  • Current fire compliance certificate
  • Lift inspection certificate
  • Electrical installation certificate
  • Periodic insurance valuation
  • Liability cover for common areas
  • Management agreement (multi-occupier buildings)
  • Maintenance logs (lift, fire systems, water tanks, generator)

Outputs From a Full Audit

A complete audit produces:

  1. An itemised findings list with each item rated for severity (immediate / 12-month / observation only)
  2. Photographic evidence for every finding
  3. NDT data (Schmidt hammer, Ferroscan) where structural compliance is at issue
  4. A remedial schedule with quantities and order-of-magnitude costs
  5. A clear safety verdict: safe / safe with remedials / unsafe for occupation
  6. EBK-registered engineer's sign-off

A safety audit is not a fault-finding mission. The goal is to confirm what is sound, identify what needs attention, and produce a defensible record. Most buildings pass once you know what to fix.
