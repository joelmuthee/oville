Questions, Answered

What clients ask us most

Practical answers about our engineering services, accreditation, timelines and how we engage with clients across Kenya. Did not find your question here? Get in touch, we are happy to help.

Is Oville Associates licensed and registered in Kenya?

Yes. Oville Associates is a fully registered engineering firm in Kenya. Our lead engineers are licensed by the Engineers Board of Kenya (EBK) and their practice is recognised by the Institution of Engineers of Kenya (IEK). Every report, drawing and certification we issue is signed off by a registered professional engineer, so it is acceptable to county approvers, banks, insurers and the NCA.

What services do you offer?

We work across three connected disciplines:

  • Civil & Structural Engineering, structural design for residential, commercial and high-rise developments, including steep-terrain and reinforced-concrete projects.
  • Forensic Engineering, root-cause investigations after structural failures or collapses, plus expert-witness reports for legal and insurance matters.
  • Project Management, end-to-end coordination of construction projects, from design coordination to site supervision and handover.
Which regions do you serve?

Our head office is in Milimani, Nairobi, and we deliver projects across Kenya and the wider East African region.

Within Kenya we have worked in Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu, Nakuru, Eldoret and several upcountry counties. Beyond Kenya, we have delivered assignments in South Sudan (including multiple projects in Juba), Uganda, Rwanda and Tanzania.

For sites outside Nairobi we travel with our own testing equipment so that a single visit covers inspection, sampling and on-site testing.

What is the difference between a structural assessment and a structural audit?

A structural assessment is a focused engineering review, typically used when you suspect a specific issue (cracks, settlement, a planned change of use) or want clearance before alterations. A structural audit is broader and more formal: a full health check of the building's structural system, often required by insurers, regulators, or before a purchase. Audits include non-destructive testing such as Schmidt hammer and ferroscan, and end with a signed report and a remedial action plan if needed.

What testing methods do you use on existing buildings?

We use a combination of non-destructive testing (NDT) techniques so we can evaluate a building without damaging it. These include Schmidt hammer testing for concrete strength, ferroscan for rebar location and cover, ultrasonic pulse velocity for internal defects, and where necessary, core sampling sent to certified labs. The mix of tests is chosen based on the building's age, structure type and the concern being investigated.

Do you handle forensic investigations after a building failure?

Yes, this is one of our specialised services. When a structure fails, partially collapses, or shows alarming distress, we lead the root-cause investigation: site preservation, evidence sampling, structural back-analysis, and a defensible report. Our forensic reports are accepted in insurance claims, regulatory inquiries and court proceedings, and we are available to act as expert witnesses where needed.

How long does a typical engagement take?

It depends on scope. A focused structural assessment of a single building is usually 5 to 10 working days from site visit to signed report. A full audit with NDT can take 2 to 4 weeks. Design work for a new building runs in parallel with the architect's programme, typically 4 to 12 weeks for structural drawings, depending on complexity. We share a clear timeline and milestones before we start.

How is pricing structured?

Fees follow the EBK Scale of Fees for structural design and project management, which is calculated as a percentage of construction value. For assessments, audits and forensic investigations, we quote a fixed fee based on the building size, accessibility and testing required. We share a written quotation before any work begins, with no hidden surcharges.
